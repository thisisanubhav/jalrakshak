# Source register

Access date for every web source below: **1 October 2026**. “Primary” means an issuing government or standards body; it does not mean that self-reported programme data have been independently verified. Where a publication page and a PDF differ in date metadata, this register uses the date printed in or stated by the source where available.

## Primary / authoritative

### S01 — JJM Drinking Water Quality Monitoring & Surveillance Framework

- **Organization:** Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (DDWS), Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India
- **Date:** 2021 (JJM site lists 5 January 2022)
- **URL:** https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/guideline/WQMS-Framework.pdf
- **Type:** Primary programme framework
- **Supports:** village FTK surveillance; five trained women; source and tail-end testing; WQMIS roles; FTK-to-laboratory escalation; laboratory network; alerts and remedial-action workflow.
- **Limitations:** describes the intended system, not proof that it functions uniformly in every village.

### S02 — WQMIS portal and reports

- **Organization:** DDWS, Ministry of Jal Shakti
- **Date:** Live system
- **URL:** https://ejalshakti.gov.in/WQMIS/
- **Type:** Primary operational information system
- **Supports:** existing roles for public users, FTK users, sample collectors, laboratory staff and officials; laboratory locator; lab/FTK reporting and remedial-action fields.
- **Limitations:** public totals sometimes render as zero or fail to load; this audit therefore does not treat a transient dashboard value as a Jharkhand estimate.

### S03 — WQMIS user guide

### S05 — Functionality Assessment 2024: Jharkhand State Report

- **Organization:** National Jal Jeevan Mission / DDWS
- **Date:** survey 2024; report published 2025
- **URL:** https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/FHTC_Jharkhand_State%20Report.pdf
- **Type:** Primary programme-commissioned assessment
- **Supports:** 555 sampled villages and 6,660 households; pH plus laboratory E. coli/total-coliform assessment; state-described confirmation at NABL-recognized district/state laboratories; Jalsahiya/sample-taker roles; uneven FTK availability (including 0% in sampled Sahibganj villages and 8.6% in Hazaribag); only 43.4% sampled villages reported skilled O&M manpower; district-level variation in functional service and assessed water quality.
- **Limitations:** covers sampled Har Ghar Jal villages, not every rural source; several state-snapshot fields are self-reported; its “potable” result uses only the parameters specified in that assessment and must not be generalized to full IS 10500 compliance.

### S10 — PIB: contamination in drinking-water sources

- **Organization:** Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India
- **Date:** 12 December 2022
- **URL:** https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1882782
- **Type:** Primary parliamentary-answer release
- **Supports:** state-reported Jharkhand count at that date: 2 fluoride-affected habitations (both with interim provision) and 57 iron-affected habitations; national framework uses IS 10500 benchmarks.
- **Limitations:** habitation classification is a programme inventory, not prevalence from a representative survey; it can change and conflicts in scope with local studies.

### S11 — PIB: arsenic/fluoride affected habitations

- **Organization:** Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India
- **Date:** 14 December 2023
- **URL:** https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1986268
- **Type:** Primary parliamentary-answer release
- **Supports:** Jharkhand reported two remaining fluoride-affected habitations, both covered by community purification provision, and no listed arsenic-affected habitation at that date.
- **Limitations:** does not prove absence of localized exceedances or other contaminants.

### S12 — BIS IS 10500:2012 Drinking Water — Specification

- **Organization:** Bureau of Indian Standards
- **Date:** 2012; reviewed 2023; amendments must be checked before operational use
- **URL:** https://www.bis.gov.in/other/DrinWatIS10500.pdf
- **Type:** Primary national standard
- **Supports:** acceptable/permissible framework and parameter-specific sampling/test requirements; pH 6.5–8.5 with no relaxation, turbidity 1 NTU acceptable/5 NTU permissible in absence of alternate source, and TDS 500/2,000 mg/L in the 2012 table.
- **Limitations:** a compliance standard is not automatically an appropriate field-sensor alarm rule; the current consolidated/amended edition must be obtained from BIS before field use.

### S13 — WHO TDS chemical fact sheet

- **Organization:** World Health Organization
- **Date:** 18 May 2022
- **URL:** https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/chemical-fact-sheets--total-dissolved-solids
- **Type:** Authoritative international guidance
- **Supports:** TDS is a collective measure of dissolved inorganic salts and small amounts of organic matter; WHO sets no health-based guideline value because reliable health-effect evidence is unavailable at levels found in drinking water; high TDS mainly affects acceptability.
- **Limitations:** global guidance; India’s applicable standard remains BIS IS 10500.

### S14 — WHO Guidelines for small water supplies

- **Organization:** World Health Organization
- **Date:** 15 February 2024
- **URL:** https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088740
- **Type:** Authoritative international guideline
- **Supports:** preventive risk management, sanitary inspection, independent surveillance, risk-based parameter selection, and monitoring frequency that accounts for parameter stability and seasonal variation.
- **Limitations:** must be adapted to Indian/Jharkhand governance and source types.

### S15 — WHO turbidity review

- **Organization:** World Health Organization
- **Date:** 14 February 2017
- **URL:** https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-FWC-WSH-17.01
- **Type:** Authoritative technical brief
- **Supports:** turbidity is operationally useful across the supply chain and may affect treatment/disinfection performance and acceptability; it is not an organism-specific test.
- **Limitations:** does not validate a particular low-cost turbidity module.

### S16 — USGS specific conductance field method

- **Organization:** U.S. Geological Survey
- **Date:** 2019
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.3133/tm9A6.3
- **Type:** Authoritative field-method manual
- **Supports:** conductivity is an indirect measure of collective dissolved ions; report at 25°C/specific conductance; relation to TDS is site/composition dependent; calibration, rinsing, immersion, stabilization, temperature compensation and QA checks are necessary.
- **Limitations:** not an Indian drinking-water standard and written for professional monitoring.

### S17 — US EPA sensor evaluation for contamination warning

- **Organization:** U.S. Environmental Protection Agency
- **Date:** updated 2019
- **URL:** https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1014GZ4.TXT
- **Type:** Authoritative technical evaluation
- **Supports:** generic online sensors cannot accurately identify and quantify the many possible contaminants; multi-parameter signals can detect anomalous change and trigger sampling/analysis.
- **Limitations:** distribution-system context and professional instruments differ from occasional community source measurements.

### S18 — US EPA field-instrument calibration SOP

- **Organization:** U.S. Environmental Protection Agency
- **Date:** 23 March 2017
- **URL:** https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/quality/standard-operating-procedure-calibration-field-instruments-temperature-pH-dissolved-oxygen_.html
- **Type:** Authoritative QA procedure
- **Supports:** field temperature, pH, conductivity and turbidity instruments require defined calibration and records.
- **Limitations:** EPA internal procedure; frequency and acceptance criteria need device- and pilot-specific validation.

### S19 — CGWB Jharkhand pre-monsoon groundwater-quality bulletin

- **Organization:** Central Ground Water Board, Ministry of Jal Shakti
- **Date:** Pre-monsoon 2024
- **URL:** https://gwdata.cgwb.gov.in/download/Bulletin/Jharkhand%20GWQ%20Bulletin%20Pre-monsoon%202024.pdf

## Peer-reviewed research

### S20 — Chandwara/Koderma groundwater study

- **Authors:** Singh et al.
- **Title:** Groundwater hydrochemistry and consumption patterns in Chandwara community development block of Jharkhand state in India
- **Date:** 2022
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s13201-022-01587-6
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed, open-access study
- **Supports:** local heterogeneity; among sampled groundwater, reported iron 0.02–6.64 mg/L, TDS 75.3–1,272 mg/L, 28.7% above the cited iron acceptable limit, 36% above 500 mg/L TDS, and turbidity exceedances linked to iron.
- **Limitations:** one block/39 villages; cannot estimate statewide prevalence; “potability” conclusions depend on tested parameters.

### S21 — East Singhbhum mining-region groundwater

- **Authors:** U.K. Singh, A.L. Ramanathan, V. Subramanian
- **Title:** Groundwater chemistry and human health risk assessment in the mining region of East Singhbhum, Jharkhand, India
- **Date:** 2018
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chemosphere.2018.04.060
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed study
- **Supports:** localized mining/processing and geology can be associated with Cd, Pb and Cr burdens; contaminant-specific laboratory analysis—not TDS—is required.
- **Limitations:** mining-region study; not representative of rural Jharkhand; risk-model assumptions and sampling design must be read in the full paper.

### S22 — Ranchi bacteriological study

- **Authors:** Kerketta et al.
- **Title:** Bacteriological Analysis of Drinking Water from Different Sources in and Around Ranchi, Jharkhand
- **Date:** 2015
- **URL:** https://microbiologyjournal.org/bacteriological-analysis-of-drinking-water-from-different-sources-in-and-around-ranchi-jharkhand/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed local study
- **Supports:** older local evidence of microbial risk: coliform detected in 53% and faecal streptococci in 20% of the study’s mixed-source samples.
- **Limitations:** old, limited local sampling and mixed source types; cannot estimate current statewide prevalence.

### S23 — Low-cost water-quality sensor systematic review

- **Authors:** Pino et al.
- **Title:** Low-Cost Water Quality Sensors for IoT: A Systematic Review
- **Date:** 2023
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181703/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed systematic review
- **Supports:** low-cost sensors may be useful, but relatively few studies validate against reference probes; calibration, maintenance, handling, environmental interference, fouling and reference comparison are essential.
- **Limitations:** heterogeneous applications and devices; does not validate JalRakshak hardware.

### S24 — Low-cost turbidity sensor field limitations

- **Authors:** Kelley et al.
- **Title:** Open-source, low-cost, in-situ turbidity sensor for river network monitoring
- **Date:** 2022
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209475/
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed technical study
- **Supports:** common low-cost optical modules may have poor low-NTU resolution, ambient-light interference and waterproofing limitations.
- **Limitations:** river-network application; a purpose-built handheld turbidimeter may perform differently.

### S25 — East Singhbhum metal(loid) study

- **Authors:** Singh et al.
- **Title:** Spatial distribution of metal(loid)s in groundwater of a mining dominated area
- **Date:** 2016
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/03067319.2016.1255735
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed study
- **Supports:** localized metal risks and spatial variation in a mining-dominated district.
- **Limitations:** district-specific and not evidence that every source is affected.

## Citation policy

Research documents link directly to the source used. No source in this register establishes that a low-cost EC/TDS reading proves safety, identifies a contaminant, or substitutes for laboratory analysis. Old or localized studies are labelled as such, and national evidence is not presented as Jharkhand-specific.
