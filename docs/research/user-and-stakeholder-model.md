# User and stakeholder model

## Institutional fit

The intended “trained field/community worker” model is broadly aligned with JJM, but the primary user cannot be chosen by product intuition alone. The national framework asks every village to train five people, preferably women such as ASHA, anganwadi and SHG members, for FTK surveillance and upload ([JJM WQMS Framework](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/guideline/WQMS-Framework.pdf)). Jharkhand-specific programme material documents Jal Sahiyas performing FTK/H2S testing, sanitary surveys, service monitoring and VWSC functions ([JJM case study](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_and_reports/101-glimpses-of-women-power.pdf)).

This supports Jal Sahiya as a **candidate primary user**, not a blanket claim that every Jal Sahiya has the mandate, time, device, training or incentive to operate electronic sensors. District DWSD/VWSC approval and field interviews are prerequisites.

## Role model

| Role | Candidate | Need / authority | JalRakshak relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary user** | Authorized, trained Jal Sahiya, FTK user or DWSD sample taker | Identify source, follow SOP, conduct repeat measurement, record QA and initiate approved escalation | Uses field capture only after training and written programme approval. |
| **Operational supervisor** | Block/district DWSD laboratory or WQMS coordinator | Owns training, device assignment, calibration review, data QA and sample-routing decisions | Reviews invalid/anomalous records and ensures they enter the official process. |
| **Decision maker** | DWSD/PHED engineer, DWSM/VWSC within delegated role, public-health official | Decides confirmation, interim action, communication and remediation | Receives a decision-support packet; software does not make public-health decisions. |
| **Laboratory** | Mapped district/state/block lab within JJM network; accredited/recognized for relevant scope | Receives traceable sample, applies parameter-specific method, issues report | Only source of `LAB_CONFIRMED` data; referral creation is not confirmation. |
| **Beneficiary** | People consuming water from the source | Needs reliable service, understandable communication and protection from false reassurance/alarm | Is not expected to interpret raw EC or provisional flags without an authorized worker. |
| **Governance/data owner** | Jharkhand DWSD and relevant Panchayat/VWSC under approved arrangements | Controls source identifiers, access, retention, public release and corrective action | Must approve pilot, privacy model and any WQMIS/Jhar-Jal interchange. |
| **Technical maintainer** | Named district/project technician | Calibrates/checks meters, manages standards, batteries, probe replacement and repairs | Mandatory role; cannot be silently assigned to community users. |

## Why Jal Sahiyas are plausible—but not automatically the answer

Evidence for fit:

- documented Jharkhand cases include FTK use, sanitary survey and Jhar-Jal reporting;
- the role connects community, VWSC/Panchayat and water service;
- JJM explicitly centers trained women in surveillance.

Evidence against assuming fit:

- case studies are selected examples, not a workload or capability survey;
- the 2024 Jharkhand assessment reported skilled O&M manpower in only 43.4% of sampled villages and uneven FTK availability ([state report](https://jaljeevanmission.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-12/FHTC_Jharkhand_State%20Report.pdf));
- adding calibration and electronic probe care may increase rather than reduce burden;
- authority to create an official lab sample/referral remains governed by DWSD/WQMIS, not the app.

## Pilot recruitment rule

Do not recruit generic “student volunteers” to test community drinking sources independently. Select users jointly with local DWSD/VWSC, confirm their existing role, compensate time according to approved arrangements, train to a competency checklist, and give them a named supervisor and escalation contact.

Students/NGO staff may support research logistics under authorization, but their records must be marked as research data and must not be represented as government surveillance.

## Questions that field interviews must answer

1. Who currently performs FTK tests in the target villages, and how often?
2. Which app/form do they use, and does it work offline?
3. What failure dominates: reagent/kit availability, training, time, connectivity, sample transport, laboratory turnaround or remedial follow-through?
4. Who is authorized to request and transport a confirmation sample?
5. Who would own calibration standards and replace a probe?
6. Would same-source history change a real decision, or merely add data-entry burden?
7. Can data be exported to the official system without duplicate source/sample identifiers?
8. What information can be shown to the community before laboratory confirmation?
