# Precommitted GO / PIVOT AGAIN / STOP criteria

Committed before direct stakeholder evidence. Do not weaken these criteria after interviews.

## Evidence minimum before any final GO

- At least 8 high-quality conversations across field user, programme/decision owner and laboratory/sample-flow roles, unless saturation or a decisive stop condition occurs earlier.
- At least 3 participants who personally performed recent field testing/sample collection.
- At least 1 person from the receiving laboratory/sample-confirmation pathway.
- Concrete recent examples, not only opinions about a proposed feature.
- Contradictions and role differences documented.
- Scientific plausibility remains separate from user/workflow validation.

## GO

GO requires all of the following:

1. Repeated same-source measurements matter in actual work (H1).
2. Historical comparison changes or materially supports a named legitimate decision made by an authorized role (H3).
3. Current tools make that comparison meaningfully difficult at the decision point (H2/H7).
4. Participants correctly treat EC as non-specific change—not contaminant detection—and can name required follow-up.
5. Offline retention and/or measurement QA addresses observed friction rather than hypothetical preference (H4/H5/H6).
6. A named organizational role can plausibly own calibration, standards, maintenance, replacement and supervision (H9).
7. The workflow can avoid or eliminate duplicate official entry through an accepted integration/export arrangement (H8).
8. Decision value plausibly exceeds visit-time, training, consumables, support and integration burden (H10).
9. Laboratory/decision stakeholders can consume the output and identify what action it may support.
10. No critical stop condition below applies.

## PIVOT AGAIN

Choose PIVOT AGAIN when a real, important workflow problem is validated but:

- EC is not the right or sufficient measurement;
- history matters, but the capability belongs inside Jhar-Jal/WQMIS rather than a companion;
- measurement QA is valuable but should be a training/checklist/calibration service, not another app;
- sanitary inspection, sample chain-of-custody, FTK availability, lab logistics or result return is substantially more valuable;
- the useful user is a supervisor/laboratory rather than the field worker;
- a non-hardware workflow change solves the problem more simply.

The revised concept and new assumptions must be stated before implementation.

## STOP

Stop the EC product path if any decisive condition is established:

- historical EC does not change or support a real authorized decision;
- official tools already provide adequate history/offline/QA functionality;
- field workers would reject another tool or duplicate entry is unavoidable;
- no institution accepts calibration, maintenance and replacement ownership;
- EC ambiguity prevents a defined operational action;
- the receiving official/laboratory workflow cannot consume the output;
- users interpret normal EC as proof of safety despite safeguards;
- expected benefit is lower than improving FTK/reagent/training/sample-transport capacity;
- maintaining valid measurements is infeasible at intended cost/skill level.
