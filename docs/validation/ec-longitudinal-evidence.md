# Longitudinal EC evidence review

## Question

Can repeated source-level electrical conductivity (EC) legitimately support an operational decision in Jharkhand rural-water work?

## Conclusion

**Scientifically plausible as a non-specific process/change indicator; operational value in Jharkhand is unvalidated.** Longitudinal, temperature-compensated EC can reveal changes in total ionic character and has established uses in salinity/mineralization, mixing, intrusion, groundwater-flow/process and broad anomaly monitoring. It cannot identify the cause, the contaminant or health significance. A field action is defensible only as “verify the measurement and investigate through the approved workflow.”

No reviewed authority supports a universal percentage change that means dangerous water. A useful threshold must exceed measurement uncertainty and normal source/season variability and be validated against the local decision and reference analyses.

## What EC change can legitimately indicate

### Collective hydrochemical change

Specific conductance is the collective response of dissolved ions, normalized to 25°C. It is useful for estimating overall mineralization/TDS when a local composition-specific relationship is understood, but the relationship is not universal ([USGS specific-conductance method](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm9A6.3)). A sustained within-source change can therefore indicate that the ionic mixture or concentration changed.

### Salinity or intrusion processes

Specific conductance/TDS are widely used indicators of saline-water movement. USGS describes monitoring wells as early-warning points for saltwater movement, but stresses that hydrogeology, groundwater withdrawals, water levels and boundaries are required for interpretation ([USGS freshwater–saltwater environments](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/2003/circ1262/)). This is a valid specialized use, not evidence that every inland Jharkhand EC change represents salinity intrusion.

### Process and mixing indicators

EC time series can reveal dilution, concentration, changing flow paths or human inputs. A USGS spring/groundwater study documented short precipitation-related dilution, spike-and-recovery events, longer seasonal trends and disturbances that mimicked water-quality change ([USGS, 2023](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20235107/full)). Thus the signal can be operationally useful only alongside rainfall, flow, site and QA context.

### Broad early warning

EPA contamination-warning research uses common parameters such as conductivity as non-specific anomaly signals because available sensors cannot accurately identify and quantify the many possible contaminants. An anomaly prompts sampling and analysis rather than a contaminant conclusion ([EPA sensor evaluation](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1014GZ4.TXT)). That logic supports H3 scientifically, but does not prove occasional handheld readings produce enough temporal resolution for the same benefit.

## What EC cannot explain

EC change cannot, by itself, answer:

- which ion or contaminant changed;
- whether the cause was benign geology, rainfall/recharge, pumping, mixing, repair/disinfection, agriculture, sewage or industry;
- whether arsenic, fluoride, iron, nitrate, lead, pathogens or pesticides are present;
- whether health risk rose or fell;
- whether unchanged EC means unchanged safety.

Trace hazards and microorganisms may change without a diagnostic bulk-conductivity response. The output is therefore a **measurement anomaly requiring context**, not “contamination detected.”

## Sources of real natural/operational variability

| Factor | How it can change EC | Required control/context |
|---|---|---|
| Rainfall/recharge | Dilution can lower EC; first flush or mobilized salts can cause short spikes; response and lag vary by source. | Recent rainfall, season, time since event and repeat after return to comparable conditions. |
| Seasonal hydrology | Water level, flow path, evaporation and residence time change ionic composition/concentration. | Compare like seasons; establish more than one seasonal baseline. WHO recommends monitoring timing based on seasonal risk ([WHO small-supply guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088740)). |
| Pumping and use | Standing well water may differ from formation water; drawdown/mixing can change chemistry. | Fixed source-specific flushing/pumping protocol and stabilization record. USGS ties representative well sampling to purging and stable field properties ([USGS field-measurement guidance](https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/09/a6.0/tm9a6.0.pdf)). |
| Sampling point | Source, storage, treatment outlet and tail-end tap can differ. | Immutable source plus named sampling point; never combine their baselines. |
| Temperature | Conductivity changes roughly 0.5–3% per °C depending on ions; compensation is model-dependent. | Store raw temperature/EC and compensation details; compare specific conductance at 25°C ([USGS](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm9A6.3)). |
| Repair, chlorination or source downtime | Can temporarily change flow, mixing and ionic conditions. | Record events and interpret separately. |
| Spatial heterogeneity | Nearby sources or depths may have different geology and water. | Compare a source to itself; no village-wide baseline substitution. |

## Measurement artifacts

| Artifact | Consequence | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Inadequate flushing/stabilization | Measures stored plumbing/well water rather than representative withdrawal. | Standardized pumping/flush and stability observations appropriate to the source. |
| Air bubbles / incomplete immersion | Erratic or biased sensor response. | Correct immersion and bubble removal. |
| Carryover/dirty probe | Previous sample or deposits bias the next reading. | Defined rinse/clean sequence and fresh aliquots. |
| Fouling | Progressive or abrupt bias. USGS examples required material corrections to conductivity records. | Before/after cleaning checks and invalidation rules ([USGS monitor guidance](https://pubs.usgs.gov/tm/2006/tm1D3/)). |
| Calibration drift | Instrument response moves despite unchanged water. | Traceable standards, pre/post checks, calibration ID and quarantine on failure. |
| Device/unit/factor mismatch | µS/cm vs mS/cm or differing TDS factor creates artificial change. | Store raw EC, units, device and algorithm; never compare incompatible derived TDS values. |
| Operator timing | Recording before stabilization increases variation. | Display trend/stability; capture stabilization time and duplicate sequence. |

## Discrete handheld measurements versus continuous monitoring

Much supportive literature uses continuous, professionally maintained stations. Their anomaly value comes from dense data, baseline characterization, reference checks and context. JalRakshak’s proposed occasional visits are weaker:

- short-lived events can be missed;
- visit time may coincide with pumping/rainfall artifacts;
- fewer observations make normal variability harder to estimate;
- different operators/devices can dominate the signal.

This limits transferability. The first human/field validation should ask whether a decision truly benefits from occasional repeat visits—not assume continuous-monitor findings apply.

## Implications for H1, H3, H5 and H6

- **H1:** repeated measurements are scientifically more interpretable than one-offs, but direct operational value is untested.
- **H3:** persistent EC change can legitimately trigger verification/context review and possibly approved sampling; it cannot select a contaminant or declare danger.
- **H5:** calibration/device traceability is scientifically necessary for any EC history, but may be an added burden rather than an existing pain.
- **H6:** duplicate stabilized readings help distinguish immediate instability, but whether they improve decisions enough to justify field time is untested.

## No anomaly threshold in this round

Do not use “10%,” “two standard deviations” or any other generic alarm. Even professional EPA work notes that significant-change settings depend on baseline variability and monitoring point. A future experimental rule must combine:

1. valid pre/post calibration checks;
2. within-visit agreement;
3. comparable source, sampling point, device/method and season;
4. a change larger than validated measurement uncertainty;
5. repeat confirmation;
6. documented action owned by the official workflow.

Until those exist, the only result categories are measurement-quality states and descriptive history.

## Evidence limitations

- No reviewed longitudinal EC study demonstrates decision impact for Jharkhand Jal Sahiyas or rural laboratories.
- International professional-monitoring evidence may not transfer to handheld community use.
- Jharkhand is inland, so saltwater-intrusion evidence illustrates method validity but not local priority.
- Published sensor performance does not validate any future device.
- Direct stakeholder evidence remains pending.
