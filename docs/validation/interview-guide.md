# Non-leading workflow interview and concept test

Target duration: **15–20 minutes**. Obtain consent before notes or recording. Do not show JalRakshak until Section J.

## Introduction

“I am working on a student research project to understand how rural water-quality measurements are recorded and followed up in Jharkhand. I am not evaluating your performance and I am not selling a product. I would like to learn from one recent example. Participation is voluntary; please avoid sharing personal or confidential information. May I take anonymized notes?”

## A — Role (2 minutes)

1. What is your role in water-quality monitoring?
2. How often do you personally test water or collect/receive samples?
3. What source types and geographic area do you work with?
4. Which tasks are you authorized to perform, and who supervises them?

## B — Current workflow (3 minutes)

1. Please walk me through the last water source you tested or sample you handled.
2. What prompted the visit/test?
3. What did you measure or observe, and what did you record?
4. Where did the result go? Which paper form, app or portal did you use?
5. What happened afterward? How did you know?

Ask for the concrete example again if the answer becomes a description of intended policy.

## C — Repeated testing (2 minutes)

1. Do you test the same source more than once? When and why?
2. How do you identify that it is the same source?
3. Can you see its previous values while at the source?
4. Tell me about a time an earlier result affected what you did.
5. If earlier results do not affect the decision, what does?

## D — Disagreement and measurement quality (2 minutes)

1. What happens when two field measurements disagree?
2. When do you repeat a test?
3. Is there a stabilization, flushing or waiting procedure? Please describe the last time you used it.
4. When do you stop trusting a field result?
5. What do you record about a failed or doubtful measurement?

## E — Calibration and device ownership (2 minutes)

1. Which instruments or kits do you use now?
2. Who checks or calibrates each one, and how often?
3. How can you tell when the last check occurred?
4. What happens when a device, reagent or kit appears unreliable or expires?
5. Who pays for standards, reagents, repair and replacement?

## F — Connectivity and records (2 minutes)

1. Do you enter results at the source or later?
2. What happened the last time connectivity was poor?
3. Do you write anything down and enter it again later?
4. Tell me about any result that was lost, delayed or entered twice.
5. How do you resolve duplicate source or sample records?

## G — Escalation (2 minutes)

1. What causes a repeat test, sanitary inspection or laboratory confirmation?
2. Who decides and who collects/transports the sample?
3. What evidence accompanies it?
4. What information is commonly missing or unusable when a sample is received?
5. How is the result returned, and who acts on it?

## H — Historical change (2 minutes)

1. If today’s measurement differs significantly from earlier measurements at the same source, what would you do?
2. Would the previous measurements affect that decision? How?
3. Can you currently access those measurements easily?
4. What else would you need before acting on the difference?
5. What natural or operational events might explain a change without contamination?

Do **not** ask whether an “EC anomaly detector” would be useful.

## I — Burden (1 minute)

1. What is the most time-consuming or error-prone part of this workflow?
2. What information do you enter more than once?
3. What would make an additional tool unacceptable?
4. If one part could be improved without adding a tool, what would it be?

## J — Concept test (only after discovery)

Read exactly:

> “JalRakshak would not determine whether water is safe and would not identify a contaminant. It would record repeated conductivity measurements, require duplicate readings, retain them offline, compare a source with its own history, and package a persistent change for the existing approved confirmation workflow.”

Then ask:

1. What part, if any, would help with the workflow you described?
2. What part is unnecessary or based on a wrong assumption?
3. Would this change any decision? Please name the decision and who makes it.
4. Where would it duplicate your current tools or records?
5. What would make you refuse to use it?
6. Who should own, check and calibrate the device?
7. What should happen after a persistent change?
8. Would this capability be better inside an existing system? Why?
9. What would you test before deciding whether to adopt it?

Never ask “Do you like JalRakshak?” Interest without changed behavior or decision is not validation.

## Close

Ask permission for a follow-up, whether another role sees a different part of the workflow, and whether any note should be treated as confidential. Do not request contact details in the repository; retain them only in an approved private system.
